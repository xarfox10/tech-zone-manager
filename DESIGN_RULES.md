# TECH ZONE Manager - Design Rules

## Rule #1

Never lose important business data.

---

## Rule #2

Every important action saves immediately.

Examples:

- Sale completed
- Product added
- Product edited
- Product deleted

---

## Rule #3

Offline first.

Internet is a bonus, never a requirement.

---

## Rule #4

Everything sold is an Item.

Examples:

- Product
- Service
- Repair
- Installation
- Labor
- Second-hand item

---

## Rule #5

If hardware fails, the business continues.

Examples:

- Barcode scanner broken → Search manually
- Printer unavailable → Continue selling
- Internet lost → Continue working

---

## Rule #6

The cashier should never panic because of the software.

---

## Rule #7

TECH ZONE Manager should feel like a dependable coworker.

---

## Rule #8

Simple is better than fancy.

Speed and reliability always come first.

---

## Rule #9

Every feature must solve a real business problem.

Not because it looks cool.

Because it helps the store.

## Rule #10

The Dashboard tells a story.

It is not a page for working.
It is a page for understanding the business.

The Dashboard should require almost no interaction.

Important information should be visible immediately.

Dashboard cards may gently fade every 5 seconds to reveal additional related information (such as previous week or previous month totals), but only when comparison data exists.

Animations should be subtle, calm, and never distracting.

The Dashboard shows facts, not opinions.

Use numbers, colors, and arrows instead of emotional wording.

Every widget on the Dashboard must answer one question:

"Will this help run the business better?"

## Rule #11

A product has one identity.

Editing a product updates its information.

Stock movements must preserve history instead of overwriting information.

The system should always explain WHY inventory changed, not only WHAT the current stock is.

Reports must help real business operations, especially inventory tracking, supplier history, and BIR bookkeeping.
